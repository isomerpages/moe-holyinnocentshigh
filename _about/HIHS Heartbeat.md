<h4><strong>The Good Samaritan Ethos</strong></h4>
<p>The Good Samaritan ethos lies at the heart of Holy Innocents’ High School’s
mission and shapes how we learn, lead and serve as a community. Rooted
in our values of Humility, Integrity, Respect and Love, it is a way of
seeing and responding to others — recognising the dignity and needs of
each person, crossing boundaries where necessary, and responding with compassion
and responsibility.&nbsp;</p>
<p>At HIHS, we seek to form young people who not only receive care, but also
learn to become contributors to their communities. Through their relationships,
choices and actions, students learn to look beyond themselves, take responsibility
for others and use their strengths in service of the common good. In doing
so, they grow as Good Samaritans with the Courage to Lead and Compassion
to Serve.&nbsp;</p>
<p>The Good Samaritan ethos is lived out through three interconnected thrusts:</p>
<p>Inner Work begins with the formation of the self. Students are given opportunities
to reflect on their experiences, understand themselves and grow in character.
Through platforms such as the Good Samaritan Portfolio, students take ownership
of their reflections and personal growth. The Gideon Journey extends this
formation beyond the classroom, broadening students’ perspectives and spheres
of care through overseas civic learning experiences.&nbsp;</p>
<p>Accompaniment, inspired by the Emmaus narrative, reflects our commitment
to walk alongside one another. For students, this means bringing together
academic, pastoral, Special Educational Needs and counselling support so
that each child is known, supported and able to flourish. The same spirit
extends to staff through a culture of collegiality, mentoring and strengths-based
development.&nbsp;</p>
<p>Hospitality is our commitment to creating spaces — physical, relational
and pedagogical — where people feel welcomed, valued and able to participate.
It is reflected in environments that encourage connection and collaboration,
as well as in teaching practices that respond to diverse learners. Programmes
such as the Mustard Seed Project (MuSP) extend this spirit into learning
by enabling students to connect knowledge across disciplines and apply
it to authentic issues affecting their communities.&nbsp;</p>
<p>Ultimately, the Good Samaritan ethos is more than a set of programmes
or practices. It is the shared disposition we hope to nurture across the
HIHS community: to see the person, respond with compassion, and have the
courage to act so that others may belong and flourish.</p>
